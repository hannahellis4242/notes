We saw in the previous chapter that the geometry and the physics were linked by equation \ref{eq:link}.

\subsection{Angular Momentum From Geometry}

We can rearrange equation \ref{eq:link} to get

\begin{align}
\frac{l^2}{GMm^2} &= A(1-e^2)\nonumber \\
&=\frac{A^2(1-e^2)}{A} \nonumber \\
&=\frac{B^2}{A} \nonumber \\
&=\frac{ap}{A} \nonumber \\
&=\frac{2ap}{a+p}
\end{align}

With some further rearrangement we can obtain the angular momentum per unit mass of the orbiting body

\begin{align}
\frac{l^2}{GMm^2} &=\frac{2ap}{a+p} \nonumber \\
\left(\frac{l}{m}\right)^2 &= GM \frac{2ap}{a+p} \nonumber \\
\frac{l}{m}&=\sqrt{GM  \frac{2ap}{a+p}}
\end{align}

\subsection{Total Energy From Geometry}

The total energy\footnote{eqation \ref{eq:Gravity:TotalEnergy}} is given by
\begin{equation*}
E(r,\dot{r})=\frac{1}{2}m \dot{r}^2 +  \frac{l^2}{2mr^2} - \frac{GMm}{r}
\end{equation*}
making this total energy per unit mass of the orbiting body instead
\begin{equation}
\frac{E}{m}=\frac{1}{2} \dot{r}^2 +  \frac{1}{2r^2}\left(\frac{l}{m}\right)^2 - \frac{GM}{r}
\end{equation}
If we choose one of the apses, say the periapsis we know that at this point $\dot{r}=0$ so the total energy per unit mass is
\begin{align}
\frac{E}{m}&= \frac{1}{2p^2}\left(\frac{l}{m}\right)^2 - \frac{GM}{p}\nonumber \\
&=\frac{1}{2p^2}\left(GM\frac{2ap}{a+p}\right) - \frac{GM}{p}\nonumber \\
&=GM\left[\frac{2ap}{2p^2(a+p)} - \frac{1}{p}\right]\nonumber \\
&=GM\left[\frac{2ap}{2p^2(a+p)} - \frac{2p(a+p)}{2p^2(a+p)}\right]\nonumber \\
&=GM\left[\frac{2ap-2p(a+p)}{2p^2(a+p)}\right]\nonumber \\
&=GM\left[\frac{-2p^2}{2p^2(a+p)}\right]\nonumber \\
&=-GM\left[\frac{1}{a+p}\right]\nonumber \\
&=-\frac{GM}{2}\left[\frac{2}{a+p}\right]\nonumber \\
&=-\frac{GM}{2A} \label{eq:totalEnergyGeometry}
\end{align}
Note that energy is negative because we took zero to be when the particle is at infinity. We can rearrange equation \ref{eq:totalEnergyGeometry} to obtain the semi major axis as
\begin{align}
\frac{E}{m}=\tilde{E}&=-\frac{GM}{2A}\nonumber \\
2A\tilde{E}&=-{GM}\nonumber \\
A&=-\frac{GM}{2\tilde{E}}
\end{align}
Where we are using $\tilde{E}=\frac{E}{m}$ to be the energy per unit mass of the orbiting body. This can then be fed back into our relationship for angular momentum.
\begin{align}
\tilde{l}^2 &= GMA(1-e^2)\nonumber \\
&=-\frac{(GM)^2(1-e^2)}{2\tilde{E}}\nonumber \\
-\frac{2\tilde{E}\tilde{l}^2}{(GM)^2}&=1-e^2\nonumber \\
e^2-\frac{2\tilde{E}\tilde{l}^2}{(GM)^2}&=1\nonumber \\
e^2&=1+\frac{2\tilde{E}\tilde{l}^2}{(GM)^2}\nonumber \\
e&=\sqrt{1+\frac{2\tilde{E}\tilde{l}^2}{(GM)^2}}
\end{align}
Which means we can work out the semi major axis and the ecentricity based on the physics.

\subsection{Period}
An orbiting body will complete a complete orbit when it has gone through an angle $E=2\pi$, we can then use Kepler's Equation\footnote{equation \ref{eq:KeplersEquation}} to compute the period. For ease of calculation we will assume that $t_0=0$.
\begin{align}
E-e\sin E &= kt \nonumber\\
2\pi-e\sin(2\pi) &= kT \nonumber \\
T &=\frac{2 \pi}{k}
\end{align}
Substituting equation \ref{eq:Kepler4} in for $k$, we get
\begin{equation}
T =\frac{2 \pi A^2\sqrt{1-e^2}}{\frac{l}{m}}
\end{equation}
